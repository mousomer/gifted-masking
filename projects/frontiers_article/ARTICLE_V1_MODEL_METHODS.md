# Formal Model and Methods

## Modelling objective and methodological position

This study asks how finite experience and progressively richer social-learning structures can shift learned behaviour away from authenticity, including toward concealment, even when the corresponding full-information objective benchmark in the specified model continues to favour authenticity. The study is an existence-and-structure computational modelling analysis. Its purpose is to expose distinctions that can be defined exactly in simplified environments, not to estimate the numerical psychology of gifted children or the prevalence of any behaviour in a human population.

The models abstract qualitative features that are relevant to visibility management: concealment can carry a continuing cost, adverse social responses can be costly, receptivity can differ between contexts, and a receptive high-performing context can still impose comparison costs. These relations motivate the structures considered below. The numerical parameters instantiate those structures on a normalized reference scale. No payoff, learning, or transition parameter is an empirically calibrated estimate, and a model step has no calibrated interpretation as a day, week, interaction, or developmental interval. The simplified construction is methodologically useful because learned behaviour can be compared with precisely defined objective benchmarks while one structural feature at a time is made explicit.

The formal progression contains four models. M0 is a two-action risky-authenticity bandit. M1 introduces graded disclosure without changing the static endpoint optimum. M2 introduces persistent social states and therefore a dynamic objective. M3 introduces heterogeneous contexts that must be discovered and, within the same model, a comparison cost attached to accepted authenticity in a receptive context. The models form a logical rather than developmental sequence. M2 and M3 are distinct controlled abstractions: M2 concerns the future value of persistent state, whereas M3 concerns discovery across contexts. Endogenous receptivity is outside the evidential scope of this article.

## Three meanings of value

Three quantities must remain distinct throughout the analysis.

**Environmental or objective reward** is the scalar reward generated by the specified environment. It is the quantity used to define expected payoff, objective pseudo-regret, and the full-information comparators. “Objective” means objective relative to the stated reward and transition equations. It does not mean human welfare, moral desirability, a psychologically true utility, or a prescription that a gifted child ought to behave in a particular way.

**Learner-processed reward and learned value** are the quantities used by the finite learner. A realized environmental reward may be transformed before updating, and the learner's action values summarize its sampled experience rather than full knowledge of the environment. The baseline negative-reward weight is \(\lambda=1\), so the baseline learner is loss-neutral under the maintained transform. Baseline findings are therefore not consequences of explicit loss aversion.

**The full-information objective benchmark** is the action or policy that maximizes the relevant environmental-reward criterion within the specified model while knowing the payoff and, where relevant, transition structure. In M0, M1, and M3 this is a static expected-reward comparison. In M2 it is an infinite-horizon discounted Markov decision process. These comparators are oracles: they possess information unavailable to the finite learner. The analysis does not decompose a learner-oracle difference into separately identified information, exploration, finite-horizon, or update-efficiency components.

## Notation and reference scale

Table 1 gives the notation used in the formal models. The symbols are computational quantities. They are not asserted to be directly measurable psychological constructs.

| Symbol | Model role |
|---|---|
| \(b\) | belonging or ordinary social-participation benefit |
| \(a\) | value assigned to authentic expression, received under either authentic outcome |
| \(c\) | continuing cost of concealment or masking |
| \(r\) | cost of a rejected authentic action |
| \(p\) | receptivity or probability that authenticity is accepted |
| \(\alpha\) | constant learning rate |
| \(\tau\) | softmax temperature |
| \(\lambda\) | multiplicative weight applied to negative realized rewards |
| \(q_0\) | initial action value |
| \(\gamma\) | M2 discount factor |
| \(T\) | number of model steps in a learner episode |
| \(n\) | number of simulated learners in a parameter cell |
| \(d\) | M1 disclosure level |
| \(b_2,r_2\) | M2 stigmatized-state masking baseline and rejection cost |
| \(\delta\) | M2 turnover probability from Accepted to Neutral |
| \(\eta\) | M2 escape probability from Stigmatized to Neutral |
| \(K\) | number of M3 contexts |
| \(p_{\mathrm{good}},p_{\mathrm{bad}}\) | receptivity in receptive and unreceptive M3 contexts |
| \(bf\) | M3 comparison cost on accepted authenticity in a receptive context |

The shared reference payoffs are

\[
(b,a,c,r)=(1.0,0.6,0.25,2.0).
\]

They define a normalized reference realization rather than fitted psychological magnitudes. Exact values are retained in the experimental specifications because reproducibility requires them; detailed provenance and numerical rationale belong to the parameter appendix.

Behaviour depends jointly on reward scale, initialization, and temperature. For any \(s>0\), the maintained learner is pathwise equivalent under

\[
(R,q_0,\tau)\mapsto(sR,sq_0,s\tau),
\]

provided that every reward-dimension quantity is scaled together—including \(b,a,c,r,b_2,r_2,bf\)—while \(\alpha,\lambda,\gamma\), all probabilities, and the random-number streams remain fixed. The result follows from positive homogeneity of the reward transform, linearity of the value update, and invariance of softmax probabilities when \(Q\) and \(\tau\) are scaled together. It is not an unconditional scale invariance: absolute value-iteration and action-tie tolerances of \(10^{-10}\) do not scale covariantly. Consequently, quantities such as a reward gap in temperature units, \(\Delta R/\tau\), can be more informative about behavioural discrimination than a raw reward difference alone.

The analysis distinguishes three levels of evidential statement. Structural or analytic statements follow from the model equations, such as the M0 boundary, the affine M1 objective, the M2 Bellman formulation, and the one-good-context M3 invariance. Tested-axis statements describe behaviour over an explicitly enumerated grid or curve, such as \((p,\eta)\), \(K\), or \(bf\). Reference-configuration statements concern quantities that were held at one value. Varying one or two axes does not turn the unswept settings into globally robust results. This distinction is especially important for a normalized but uncalibrated parameterization: an exact derivation may be general in symbols even when its numerical realization is conditional on reference inputs, whereas a simulation curve may cover many cells while remaining conditional on every parameter it did not vary.

### Parameter governance and domain conventions

Parameters enter the study in three roles. **Structural parameters** define a model's mechanism, such as the linear disclosure function in M1, the transition quantities \(\delta\) and \(\eta\) in M2, and the accepted-good-context placement of \(bf\) in M3. **Reference coordinates** select the common numerical realization used to compare mechanisms, including the payoff vector, learner settings, and reference receptivities. **Tested coordinates** are explicitly varied by an experiment and have enumerated values below. A value can occupy more than one role—for example, \(\eta\) is structural to M2 and is also a tested E03 coordinate—but these labels prevent a fixed reference choice from being mistaken for an empirically estimated constant.

Probability inputs are interpreted on their closed mathematical domains. Receptivity values satisfy \(0\leq p\leq1\); M2 turnover and escape probabilities satisfy \(0\leq\delta,\eta\leq1\). Temperature must be strictly positive, the learning rate is constrained to \(0<\alpha\leq1\), and the discounted M2 comparator requires \(0\leq\gamma<1\). The maintained learner permits \(\lambda=0\), which removes negative rewards from the immediate update signal, but rejects \(\lambda<0\) because it would reverse the sign of losses rather than represent attenuation. These computational domain rules do not supply empirical support for all values within the domain.

The reference settings were specified before interpretation of the standard artifacts. Sensitivity coordinates were likewise prespecified in the experiment protocols and are reported only when their preserved outputs exist. A planned condition is not treated as executed merely because the simulator can represent it. No parameter is adjusted in the manuscript to strengthen a pattern, move a learned crossing, or align a simulation with its analytic comparator. The later Results chapter must therefore distinguish an analytic change implied by an equation, an observed change over an executed axis, and an untested hypothesis about another coordinate.

The benchmark criterion is also governed by model class. Static bandit comparators maximize environmental expected reward for a single action class under complete knowledge. The M2 comparator maximizes expected discounted environmental reward over stationary state-conditioned policies. Neither comparator is information-matched to the learner, and neither incorporates the reward transform when \(\lambda\ne1\). Objective pseudo-regret is thus a formal distance from an oracle under the stated environmental reward; it is not a measure of blame, irrationality, clinical impairment, or the welfare cost of a person's adaptation. Where the action set is graded or context-indexed, the comparator uses precisely that model's action set rather than importing a preferred action from another rung.

## Shared finite learner

### Learner-processed reward

For a realized environmental reward \(R\), the maintained transform is

\[
v_\lambda(R)=
\begin{cases}
R, & R\geq 0,\\
\lambda R, & R<0.
\end{cases}
\]

The implementation requires \(\lambda\geq0\). The transform changes only the learner update; it never changes the environmental reward or an objective comparator. At the reference value \(\lambda=1\), \(v_\lambda(R)=R\) for all rewards. Values above one give negative outcomes greater update weight, whereas values between zero and one attenuate them.

For the stateless or context-bandit models, only the selected action value is updated:

\[
Q_t(x)\leftarrow Q_t(x)+\alpha\left[v_\lambda(R_t)-Q_t(x)\right].
\]

Unselected values are unchanged. M1 assigns a separate tabular value to every disclosure level. The flat M3 architecture assigns a separate value to every context-behaviour pair. Model-specific departures from this representation are stated below.

For a stateless action \(x\) with mean transformed reward \(\mu_x=\mathbb E[v_\lambda(R_x)]\), the conditional expected update is

\[
\mathbb E[\Delta Q(x)\mid x\text{ selected},Q(x)]
=\alpha[\mu_x-Q(x)].
\]

Thus \(\mu_x\) is the conditional mean-reversion target if the action continues to be sampled. With constant \(\alpha\) and stochastic rewards, this identity is not a proof that the realized Q process converges to a point. Selection and learning are coupled: a low estimated value reduces future sampling, which reduces opportunities for corrective evidence. This action-dependent information channel is the minimal learning feature used in M0 and remains present in the richer models.

### Action selection

At each model step, the learner samples from all actions available in its current representation using

\[
\Pr(X_t=x)=
\frac{\exp\{Q_t(x)/\tau\}}
{\sum_{x'}\exp\{Q_t(x')/\tau\}},\qquad \tau>0.
\]

The implementation subtracts the row-wise maximum of \(Q/\tau\) before exponentiation, which is algebraically equivalent and numerically stable. Every finite-valued action therefore retains positive sampling probability. “Settling” in the experimental definitions below describes final-window modal behaviour, not an absorbing state or a proof of asymptotic convergence.

For any two actions \(i\) and \(j\), softmax odds satisfy

\[
\frac{\Pr(i)}{\Pr(j)}
=\exp\left(\frac{Q(i)-Q(j)}{\tau}\right).
\]

Temperature controls how strongly a learned value gap changes sampling, but it does not supply knowledge of the true reward. For exactly two actions, a softmax probability of one half occurs at equal Q values. For more than two actions, the probability of one action depends on all alternatives. In particular,

\[
\Pr(i)=\frac12
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
Q(i)=\tau\log\sum_{j\ne i}\exp\{Q(j)/\tau\}.
\]

This multi-action condition is distinct from both pairwise Q equality and the across-learner modal fraction used in E05.

All values are initialized to \(q_0=0\) unless an explicit sensitivity condition states otherwise. This initialization is not behaviourally neutral relative to the reference payoffs: it lies below the deterministic masking reward \(b-c=0.75\) and above the rejected-authenticity reward \(a-r=-1.4\). The shared reference learner uses \(\alpha=0.1\), \(\tau=0.25\), \(\lambda=1\), and \(q_0=0\). M2 additionally uses \(\gamma=0.9\).

### Time and final-window summaries

One simulation step consists operationally of action selection, generation of the environment outcome and reward, and the corresponding selected-value update; M2 also generates a next state. It is not mapped to chronological human time. Episodes have a finite horizon \(T\). A final-window fraction \(f=0.2\) is implemented as a window of \(\lceil fT\rceil\) steps ending at \(T-1\). The standard experiments therefore use steps 1600–1999 when \(T=2000\), and steps 800–999 when \(T=1000\). Final-window quantities summarize behaviour near the end of the episode. They do not assert convergence to an asymptotic policy.

## M0: minimal risky-authenticity bandit

M0 contains two actions: masking or concealment, denoted \(M\), and authenticity, denoted \(A_u\). Masking has deterministic reward

\[
R_M=b-c.
\]

Authenticity is accepted with probability \(p\):

\[
R_{A_u}=
\begin{cases}
b+a, & \text{with probability }p,\\
a-r, & \text{with probability }1-p.
\end{cases}
\]

Its objective expected reward is

\[
\mathbb E[R_{A_u}]
=p(b+a)+(1-p)(a-r)
=a+pb-(1-p)r.
\]

The objective authenticity-minus-masking gap is

\[
\Delta_0(p)
=\mathbb E[R_{A_u}]-R_M
=a+c-(1-p)(b+r).
\]

For \(b+r>0\), the general objective indifference boundary is

\[
p^*_{\mathrm{obj}}
=1-\frac{a+c}{b+r}.
\]

Authenticity has greater objective expected reward above this boundary and masking below it. Under the reference payoff parameterization,

\[
R_M=\frac34,\qquad
\mathbb E[R_{A_u}]=-\frac75+3p,
\]

so

\[
p^*_{\mathrm{obj}}=\frac{43}{60}\approx0.7167.
\]

The fraction \(43/60\) is an exact result conditional on the reference payoffs, not a universal psychological threshold. M0 is deliberately minimal: it has no persistent environmental state, contextual heterogeneity, graded disclosure, feedback from behaviour to receptivity, strategic opponent, or planning over social-state dynamics. It separates finite stochastic learning from the static objective comparison without claiming comprehensive realism.

The negative-reward transform also defines a learner-side expected-update boundary that must not be confused with the objective one. In the sign regime \(b-c\geq0\), \(b+a\geq0\), and \(a-r<0\), masking and accepted authenticity are unchanged by the transform, whereas rejected authenticity is multiplied by \(\lambda\). The transformed-reward mean for authenticity is

\[
\mu_{A_u}^{\lambda}
=p(b+a)+(1-p)\lambda(a-r),
\]

and its equality with the positive masking reward occurs at

\[
p^*_{\lambda}
=\frac{(b-c)-\lambda(a-r)}{(b+a)-\lambda(a-r)},
\]

when the denominator is positive. At \(\lambda=1\), this reduces exactly to \(p^*_{\mathrm{obj}}\). When \(r>a\) and \(a+c>0\), the learner-side boundary increases with \(\lambda\). This is an expected transformed-reward relation, not a closed-form prediction of the finite-horizon behavioural crossing. It clarifies why changing \(\lambda\) can alter learning while leaving objective environmental rewards unchanged.

## M1: graded disclosure with independent action values

M1 replaces the binary action with disclosure \(d\in[0,1]\), implemented on a finite grid that contains the endpoints exactly. Here \(d=0\) is the masking endpoint and \(d=1\) is full authenticity. The reference model assumes the linear acceptance relation

\[
q(d)=\Pr(\text{accept}\mid d)=1-(1-p)d.
\]

The action's non-social base reward is

\[
g(d)=ad-c(1-d).
\]

Acceptance produces \(g(d)+b\), and rejection produces \(g(d)-r\). Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[R(d)]
&=q(d)[g(d)+b]+[1-q(d)][g(d)-r]\\
&=b-c+d\left[a+c-(1-p)(b+r)\right]\\
&=b-c+d\,\Delta_0(p).
\end{aligned}
\]

The endpoint checks are exact: \(\mathbb E[R(0)]=b-c\), and \(\mathbb E[R(1)]\) equals the M0 full-authenticity expectation. Because the function is affine in \(d\), every strict full-information static optimum is an endpoint. Full authenticity is optimal when \(\Delta_0(p)>0\), masking when \(\Delta_0(p)<0\), and all levels are indifferent only when \(\Delta_0(p)=0\). Intermediate actions can change outcome variance, sampling allocation, and finite learning, but they do not create an interior static optimum in this reference model.

The linear form of \(q(d)\) is a stylized first abstraction rather than an empirical law. The standard M1 learner uses five independent tabular arms,

\[
d\in\{0,0.25,0.50,0.75,1\}.
\]

An observation at one level updates only that level; it does not generalize to a neighbouring level. M1 is therefore neither a continuous-function learner nor a model of smooth “ladder climbing.” This independent-arm assumption materially separates the representation tested here from a learner that exploits the ordinal geometry of disclosure.

## M2: persistent social consequences

### States, rewards, and transition order

M2 returns to the two actions \(M\) and \(A_u\) and introduces three explicit environment states: Neutral \(N\), Accepted \(A\), and Stigmatized \(S\). These are imposed model states, not measured psychological categories, and their longitudinal persistence is not empirically calibrated. Table 2 specifies the complete reference transition model.

| Current state and action | Current reward | Next-state distribution |
|---|---|---|
| \(N,M\) | \(b-c\) | \(N\) with probability 1 |
| \(N,A_u\), accepted | \(b+a\) | \(A\) with probability \(p\) |
| \(N,A_u\), rejected | \(a-r\) | \(S\) with probability \(1-p\) |
| \(A,M\) | \(b-c\) | \(N\) with \(\delta\); \(A\) with \(1-\delta\) |
| \(A,A_u\) | \(b+a\) | \(N\) with \(\delta\); \(A\) with \(1-\delta\) |
| \(S,M\) | \(b_2-c\) | \(N\) with \(\eta\); \(S\) with \(1-\eta\) |
| \(S,A_u\), redeemed | \(b+a\) | \(A\) with probability \(p/2\) |
| \(S,A_u\), not redeemed | \(a-r_2\) | \(N\) with \((1-p/2)\eta\); \(S\) with \((1-p/2)(1-\eta)\) |

The model uses reward-first ordering. Reward is generated from the current state and current outcome. Accepted-state turnover \(\delta\) then determines the next state without altering the current reward. From Stigmatized, a successful authentic redemption receives the full \(b+a\) reward and moves to Accepted. If authenticity fails, the failed reward \(a-r_2\) is generated before the same exogenous \(\eta\) escape rule determines whether the next state is Neutral or remains Stigmatized. Masking in Stigmatized also uses \(\eta\) only for the next state. Masking in Neutral never changes state.

Equivalently, using state order \((N,A,S)\), the action-conditioned masking transition matrix is

\[
P_M=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
\delta&1-\delta&0\\
\eta&0&1-\eta
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The authenticity rows are

\[
P(A_u\mid N)=(0,p,1-p),
\qquad
P(A_u\mid A)=(\delta,1-\delta,0),
\]

and

\[
P(A_u\mid S)=((1-p/2)\eta,p/2,(1-p/2)(1-\eta)).
\]

The final row reflects conditional ordering: redemption is resolved first; only a failed redemption can be followed by exogenous escape. Expected rewards and next states are paired at the event level in the comparator, so the full accepted reward is attached only to the \(S\to A\) redemption branch.

Several assumptions are deliberately visible rather than hidden in implementation. One rejected authentic action from Neutral enters Stigmatized deterministically conditional on rejection. Redemption probability is exactly \(p/2\). Successful redemption restores the full accepted payoff and the Accepted state. These are structural reference assumptions, not empirically established transition laws.

The reference M2 environment uses \(b_2=0.5\), \(r_2=2.5\), and \(\delta=0.02\). Its expected one-step reward rows are

\[
\begin{aligned}
r_N(M)&=b-c,& r_N(A_u)&=p(b+a)+(1-p)(a-r),\\
r_A(M)&=b-c,& r_A(A_u)&=b+a,\\
r_S(M)&=b_2-c,& r_S(A_u)&=(p/2)(b+a)+(1-p/2)(a-r_2).
\end{aligned}
\]

### State-action learner

The learner stores a \(3\times2\) table. After observing reward \(R_t\) and next state \(s_{t+1}\), it updates only the selected state-action pair:

\[
Q(s_t,x_t)\leftarrow Q(s_t,x_t)+\alpha\left[
v_\lambda(R_t)+\gamma\max_{x'}Q(s_{t+1},x')-Q(s_t,x_t)
\right].
\]

Actions are selected by a two-action softmax within the current state. The baseline uses \(\gamma=0.9\) and \(\lambda=1\).

### Full-information dynamic comparator

The M2 objective comparator is the optimal stationary policy of the infinite-horizon discounted MDP defined by Table 2, evaluated using untransformed environmental rewards. For \(0\leq\gamma<1\), its Bellman operator is

\[
(\mathcal TV)(s)=\max_x\left\{r(s,x)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,x)V(s')\right\}.
\]

The operator is a \(\gamma\)-contraction and therefore has a unique fixed point \(V^*\). The comparator action values are

\[
Q^*(s,x)=r(s,x)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,x)V^*(s').
\]

Value iteration starts from zero, stops when the maximum absolute change in \(V\) is at most \(10^{-10}\), and permits at most 100,000 iterations. Actions within \(10^{-10}\) of the state maximum are treated as objective ties. Convergence is required. The implementation was also checked deterministically by evaluating the relevant stationary policies through

\[
V^\pi=(I-\gamma P^\pi)^{-1}r^\pi.
\]

Because both actions have the same next-state distribution from Accepted,

\[
Q(A,A_u)-Q(A,M)=a+c>0,
\]

so authenticity is strictly dominant there under the reference sign condition. This reduces the relevant deterministic stationary policy enumeration from eight policies to four.

The Neutral action difference makes the conceptual departure from M0 explicit:

\[
D_N(V)=Q(N,A_u;V)-Q(N,M;V)
=\Delta_0(p)+\gamma\left[pV_A+(1-p)V_S-V_N\right].
\]

M2 can therefore prefer a different objective action from M0 because authenticity changes the distribution of future states as well as immediate reward. The comparator is nevertheless a full-information oracle. It is not the best policy available under observation and sample constraints matched to the learner.

The dynamic comparator and learner share the numerical discount factor \(\gamma\), but they use it differently. The comparator applies \(\gamma\) to the known transition model; the learner applies it to the largest currently learned next-state value. Changing \(\gamma\) changes both the learner's update target and the objective against which it is assessed. A different learner-objective classification after a \(\gamma\) manipulation therefore cannot automatically be read as an improvement or deterioration in learning, because the target policy may itself have changed.

## M3: heterogeneous-context discovery

### Environment and objective reward classes

M3 removes the persistent M2 state process and instead supplies \(K\) contexts. In the primary construction, one context is receptive with probability \(p_{\mathrm{good}}\), and \(K-1\) contexts have receptivity \(p_{\mathrm{bad}}\), where \(p_{\mathrm{good}}>p_{\mathrm{bad}}\). Masking has the same reward in every context. Authenticity uses the selected context's receptivity. The reference probabilities \(0.85\) and \(0.20\) instantiate a clear receptive/unreceptive contrast; they are not estimates of human acceptance rates.

With one good context, the three distinct expected-reward classes are

\[
R_M=b-c,
\]

\[
R_G(bf)=p_{\mathrm{good}}(b+a-bf)+(1-p_{\mathrm{good}})(a-r),
\]

and

\[
R_B=p_{\mathrm{bad}}(b+a)+(1-p_{\mathrm{bad}})(a-r).
\]

The comparison cost \(bf\) is subtracted only if authentic expression is both accepted and made in a good context. It is not charged after rejection, in bad contexts, or for masking. This placement is a stylized reference assumption and makes the good-context expected reward decline with slope \(-p_{\mathrm{good}}\).

Operationally, a flat joint action has index \(2g+h\), where \(g\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}\) and \(h=0\) denotes masking while \(h=1\) denotes authenticity. The environment draws an acceptance uniform using the selected context's receptivity even on masking trials, preserving a fixed random-number draw order, although the draw has no effect on a masking reward. On an authentic trial in a good context, acceptance yields \(b+a-bf\) and rejection yields \(a-r\). In a bad context, the accepted reward is \(b+a\) and the rejected reward remains \(a-r\). Each selected value is updated from the realized outcome using the common learner rule.

For fixed \(p_{\mathrm{good}},p_{\mathrm{bad}},bf\), and payoff parameters, increasing \(K\) in the one-good construction changes only the multiplicity of masking and bad-context authentic actions. The set of distinct objective values remains \(\{R_M,R_G,R_B\}\), and

\[
\max\{R_M,R_G,R_B\}
\]

is therefore invariant to \(K\). This result assumes that one unchanged good context remains available, masking is context-invariant, no direct cost is assigned to having more contexts, and the benchmark has full information. It does not generalize automatically to environments in which context quality, access, or rewards change with \(K\). Moreover, in the one-good experimental construction, the good-context fraction is exactly \(1/K\). Increasing \(K\) therefore couples a larger action/search space to greater receptive-context scarcity; it does not independently identify an intrinsic causal effect of context count.

### Action representations

Four representations are used in the M3 experiments.

1. **Flat \(2K\).** Each context-behaviour combination is a separate action. Joint action \(2g\) masks in context \(g\), and joint action \(2g+1\) is authentic in that context.
2. **Hierarchical.** A \(K\)-way softmax selects a context, after which a two-way softmax selects masking or authenticity within that context. The reference implementation updates both the selected context value and the selected within-context behaviour value using the same realized reward. This dual credit assignment is a stylized architectural choice without a unique empirical or theoretical justification.
3. **Global mask.** One shared masking action competes with \(K\) context-specific authentic actions, producing \(K+1\) actions. This is a shared-mask representation control; it also changes action cardinality, initial probability mass, competition, and update allocation.
4. **Fixed known good.** Context choice is removed, leaving a two-action masking/authenticity problem at \(p_{\mathrm{good}}\). It isolates a known receptive context and is structurally invariant to the nominal \(K\) design axis.

These are repeated abstract context/action choices with learned values. They do not model literal movement among human peer groups, context availability constraints, or a strategic search process.

When more than one good context is supplied as an E04 control, every designated good context receives \(p_{\mathrm{good}}\) and every remaining context receives \(p_{\mathrm{bad}}\). The objective comparator then treats all authentic actions in good contexts as tied when good-context authenticity is optimal, and all representation-visible masking actions as tied when masking is optimal. Random-per-learner identity changes which indices are good without changing the reward classes. These controls vary abundance or label identity; they do not add a new model rung.

### Objective comparison-cost crossover

For \(p_{\mathrm{good}}>0\), good-context authenticity and masking are objectively indifferent at

\[
bf^*_{G\mathrm{v}M}
=\frac{
p_{\mathrm{good}}(b+a)+(1-p_{\mathrm{good}})(a-r)-(b-c)
}{p_{\mathrm{good}}}
=\frac{a+c-(1-p_{\mathrm{good}})(b+r)}{p_{\mathrm{good}}}.
\]

At the reference values,

\[
R_G(bf)=\frac{23}{20}-\frac{17}{20}bf,
\qquad R_M=\frac34,
\]

and therefore

\[
bf^*_{G\mathrm{v}M}=\frac8{17}\approx0.470588.
\]

The fraction \(8/17\) is specific to the reference model and to the accepted-good-authentic placement of \(bf\). Bad-context authenticity crosses good-context authenticity only at \(39/17\) under the same reference values, so it is not the competing objective optimum over the E05 range.

## Formal progression

Table 3 summarizes the controlled change introduced by each model.

| Model | New structural element | Objective benchmark | New question |
|---|---|---|---|
| M0 | risky authenticity | static expected reward | Can finite learning diverge from static objective value? |
| M1 | graded, independently valued actions | affine static objective with endpoint optima | Does action representation alter learned behaviour without changing the static optimum? |
| M2 | persistent social states | full-information discounted MDP | Can finite learning diverge from a dynamic optimal policy? |
| M3 | heterogeneous contexts and comparison cost | full-information best context/action | Can a valuable receptive context remain under-discovered, and can learned and objective cost transitions differ? |

## Experimental methods

### Common computational design, replication, and uncertainty

All standard experiments use the shared reference payoffs and learner unless a listed condition changes one quantity explicitly. A master seed defines an independent top-level replication. Within a master, semantic cell identifiers are hashed and supplied to NumPy `SeedSequence` namespaces to derive local vectorized streams. This prevents reliance on global random state and isolates cells from execution order. Simulated learners within a cell share the cell's vectorized stream and are not treated as independent top-level replications. The five master seeds, rather than the \(n\) learner rows, are the inferential units.

The seed namespaces are experiment-specific. E01 derives separate policy/environment and intervention streams from each semantic cell. E02 derives one paired stream for the two architectures at a given master and \(p\). E03 derives each cell stream from its complete semantic identity, with explicitly approved prefix coupling only for horizon and window diagnostics. E04 separates policy/environment streams from random good-context-identity streams. E05 derives an architecture-specific master stream and deliberately reinitializes it across \(bf\) values to form a matched response curve. Runs use one worker, and output rows are sorted deterministically. These choices preserve reproducibility while preventing accidental dependence on parameter-cell execution order.

E01, E02, E04, and scalar E05 effects use Student-\(t\) intervals across five master-seed estimates with four degrees of freedom. E05 crossing uncertainty uses a top-level bootstrap of complete master-seed response curves. E03 reports cell classification and stability across the five masters; identical selected-master classifications do not imply absence of Monte Carlo or model uncertainty. Deterministic analytic quantities receive no simulation interval.

For a scalar estimate \(z_j\) from master seed \(j\), the reported centre is \(\bar z=\sum_{j=1}^{5}z_j/5\), and the two-sided interval is

\[
\bar z\;\pm\;t_{.975,4}\frac{s_z}{\sqrt{5}},
\]

where \(s_z\) is the sample standard deviation of the five master estimates. This interval quantifies between-master Monte Carlo variation under the fixed computational design. It does not quantify structural-model uncertainty, parameter uncertainty, or uncertainty about human populations. Resampling learner rows as if they were five hundred independent replications would answer a different and over-precise question, because the designed top-level replication unit is the master seed.

### E01: controlled early feedback in M0

E01 uses the M0 environment with primary receptivity cells \(p\in\{0.75,0.85\}\), both above the reference objective boundary, and the loss-neutral learner. At step 0 the policy draw and acceptance uniform are generated in the maintained order, after which the executed action is forced to authenticity. Learners are stratified by whether that initial authentic action was accepted or rejected. The primary comparison is rejected minus accepted, pooled with equal weight across the two \(p\) cells within each master seed.

This design conditions on a realized stochastic outcome; it does not assign learners to “accepted” and “rejected” environments and does not change \(p\) between the two strata. Generating the usual policy and environment draws before overriding the executed action preserves the maintained random-number schedule. The policy draw at step 0 is recorded but cannot affect the forced action, whereas the acceptance uniform determines stratum membership and the reward used in the first update. Subsequent differences can therefore be described as conditional path dependence following one controlled authentic exposure, but not as a randomized causal estimate of receiving rejection in a human population. Equal weighting of the two primary \(p\) cells is a prespecified computational summary rather than weighting by their stratum sizes or an external prevalence distribution.

Later authentic sampling is the authentic-action rate over steps 1–1999. Final masking is the masking rate over steps 1600–1999. Cumulative objective pseudo-regret at step \(t\) is the expected reward of the objectively best M0 action minus the expected reward of the selected action; normalized regret divides the cumulative sum by \(T\) times the absolute endpoint gap. In the primary authenticity-optimal binary cells, normalized regret is exactly the fraction of masking actions. Because the initial action is authentic in both strata,

\[
\Delta\mathrm{Regret}_{\mathrm{norm}}
=-\frac{T-1}{T}\Delta\overline A_{1:T-1}.
\]

Sampling and normalized regret are therefore one behavioural shift expressed in two units rather than independent outcomes.

Two secondary information manipulations were executed in the same \(p\) cells. The forced-exposure condition overrides a sampled masking action with authenticity on 5% of post-initial steps, using an intervention stream separate from the policy/environment stream. The counterfactual-feedback condition leaves the executed action unchanged but, on a masking trial, also updates the authentic value using the authentic reward that would have followed from the already drawn acceptance event; this counterfactual signal is excluded from realized objective return. Additional executed learner-weight cells compare \(\lambda=.5\) and \(2.5\) at \(p=.75\) under controlled initial exposure and at the maintained \(p=.65\) baseline. These manipulations alter information or learner processing, not the environmental objective payoff. Planned horizon and forced-exposure-rate sensitivities were not executed and are not presented as performed.

Each E01 semantic cell derives a policy/environment seed and a separate intervention seed from its master seed. The child-level output records action counts, initial outcome, final values, realized reward, and pseudo-regret; master-level stratum contrasts are the replicated estimates.

### E02: binary versus graded independent actions

E02 compares a binary endpoint grid \(\{0,1\}\) with the five-level independent grid \(\{0,.25,.5,.75,1\}\) at \(p\in\{.65,.75,.85\}\). The \(.65\) cell lies below the reference static boundary, whereas \(.75\) and \(.85\) lie above it. At step 0, the policy and acceptance draws are generated before the executed action is forced to \(d=1\). Outcomes are stratified as accepted or rejected according to that full-authentic action.

Within a master seed and \(p\) cell, the binary and graded architectures use the same derived policy/environment stream. This matched common-random-number construction permits paired architecture contrasts while leaving the selected-arm updates specific to each architecture. The primary reported stratum for architecture contrasts is the rejected initial-outcome stratum, pooled with equal weight across \(p=.75\) and \(.85\).

Nonzero disclosure is the rate of \(d>0\) over post-initial steps. Full-authentic endpoint selection is the rate of \(d=1\) in the final window. Modal-endpoint selection requires a unique final-window modal arm and asks whether it is the objective endpoint. Objective pseudo-regret sums the expected endpoint optimum minus \(\mathbb E[R(d_t)]\), normalized by \(T\) times the endpoint gap. In the authenticity-optimal affine cells,

\[
\mathrm{normalized\ regret}(d)=1-d,
\]

so horizon-average normalized regret is exactly mean distance from the full-authentic endpoint. It is an objective endpoint-distance measure, not independent evidence of psychological welfare. Only the two- and five-level architectures were executed; the planned three- and nine-level sensitivity was not.

### E03: persistent-state regime classification

E03 tests the reference M2 environment on the Cartesian grid

\[
p\in\{0,.05,\ldots,1\},\qquad
\eta\in\{0,.05,\ldots,.80\}.
\]

The 21 by 17 grid contains 357 prespecified computational cells. It is a tested domain with a uniform counting convention, not a probability distribution over real environments or a natural measure of parameter-space volume.

For each master and cell, final-window current-state/action counts are aggregated across the 160 learners. A state's visits are adequate only if its total final-window visits are at least \(\max(100,n)\) and at least \(\max(16,\lceil.2n\rceil)\) learners visit it. For the Neutral-state primary classifier, this means at least 160 visits contributed by at least 32 learners. If Neutral visits are inadequate or Neutral masking and authentic counts tie, the learned action is inconclusive. Otherwise, the larger aggregate count defines learned Neutral behaviour. This is an aggregate action-count classifier, not an individual policy frequency or a Q-ordering classifier.

Counts are attributed to the current state from which an action was selected, before the outcome-dependent transition. Thus a rejected Neutral authentic action contributes to the Neutral-authentic count even though its next state is Stigmatized. This convention aligns the observed action frequency with the comparator's state-conditioned action. It also prevents a common off-by-one interpretation in which visits would be labelled by the post-action state. Accepted and Stigmatized state-action counts, final state occupancy, and final Q values are retained as diagnostic fields, but the primary regime label is deliberately based on Neutral behaviour because that is the decision point used for the prespecified learned-versus-objective comparison.

For the same cell, value iteration computes the objective Neutral Q gap. An absolute gap no greater than \(10^{-10}\) is an objective tie and is inconclusive. The primary per-master labels are:

- `learned_suboptimal_masking` when aggregate Neutral behaviour favours masking and the full-information objective action is authenticity;
- `learner_matches_objective` when the learned and objective Neutral actions agree;
- `inconclusive` when visit or tie rules prevent classification.

Other logically possible mismatch labels are retained separately and are not folded into learned-suboptimal masking. A cell is stable across seeds only if the same non-inconclusive classification appears in at least four of the five masters. The 357-cell denominator is fixed regardless of classification, so inconclusive cells remain visible.

The standard package also contains prespecified secondary diagnostics at six sentinel cells. One-factor-at-a-time alternatives were \(n=320\); \(T\in\{2000,5000\}\); final-window fractions \(.1,.4\); \(\alpha\in\{.03,.3\}\); \(\tau\in\{.1,.6\}\); \(\lambda\in\{.5,2.5\}\); \(q_0=.75\); and \(\gamma\in\{.5,.99\}\), always against the reference value of the varied factor. A separately labelled harsh boundary used \(p\in\{.70,.75,.80\}\), \(\eta=0\), \(b_2=0\), \(r_2=8\), and \(\delta=.3\). Deterministic objective thresholds at \(\eta\in\{0,.2,.8\}\) were bisected to a bracket width at most \(10^{-4}\). Bounded dyadic learned-threshold refinement allowed at most eight added \(p\) values per \(\eta\), required a single monotone adjacent action transition, and otherwise returned an explicit censored or nonmonotone status. These diagnostics do not turn unswept M2 parameters into a globally robust domain.

### E04: context-discovery architectures

E04 uses \(p_{\mathrm{good}}=.85\), \(p_{\mathrm{bad}}=.20\), and \(bf=0\) over

\[
K\in\{1,2,4,8,16\}.
\]

It crosses the four M3 representations with fixed versus random-per-learner good-context identity and three abundance requests: one good context, two good contexts (capped at \(K\)), and a proportional-25 condition with \(\max(1,\lceil K/4\rceil)\) good contexts. Conditions that are structurally identical—for example, identity when every context is good and the nominal \(K\) values of the fixed-known-good control—are canonicalized, yielding 55 distinct executable cells from the 120 requested design combinations.

In fixed-identity cells, the requested number of good contexts occupies the lowest context indices. In random-identity cells, each learner receives a uniformly sampled subset of distinct good-context indices, sorted only for deterministic storage. The learner is not directly told which contexts are good; identity affects only the environment's receptivity lookup. A learner's assigned subset remains fixed throughout its episode. The separate identity stream ensures that changing identity assignment does not silently consume the policy/environment draws that determine action selection and outcomes.

The primary per-learner outcome is one when the deterministic `argmax` of final-window joint-action counts is an authentic action in any good context, and zero otherwise. If several arms share the maximum count, `argmax` selects the first arm; the primary definition therefore permits non-unique modes. The cell response `mean_settled_any_good_auth_argmax` is the mean of this indicator across learners. “Settled on receptive-context authenticity” refers only to this operational final-window modal classification. A strict unique-modal field was retained as a diagnostic but does not replace the prespecified primary estimand.

For flat and hierarchical learners, each architecture and \(K\) has a matched policy/environment stream across abundance and identity variants, while context identities use a separately derived stream. The primary one-good fixed-identity \(K=16\) minus \(K=1\) contrast is computed within each master and then summarized across the five masters. The randomized-identity and abundance conditions test whether the response depends on one privileged label or only one receptive option. The global-mask and fixed-known-good conditions are representation controls rather than single-component causal isolations.

Increasing \(K\) in the primary one-good condition also changes the receptive-context fraction from 1 to \(1/K\). E04 therefore varies a coupled discovery problem: action-space size, receptive-context scarcity, finite allocation of samples, and representation. It does not identify \(K\) as an intrinsic cause. The standard evidence is also conditional on the single reference receptivity environment. Planned variations of \(p_{\mathrm{good}}\), \(p_{\mathrm{bad}}\), and \(\tau\) were not executed.

### E05: comparison-cost response and crossing estimator

E05 fixes \(K=4\), the good context at index 0, \(p_{\mathrm{good}}=.85\), and \(p_{\mathrm{bad}}=.20\). It evaluates the flat \(2K\) and hierarchical architectures at 18 comparison-cost values:

\[
\{0,.30,.325,.35,.375,.40,.425,.45,8/17,.475,.50,.525,.55,.575,.60,.625,.65,.80\}.
\]

The dense fitting set contains the regular \(.30\) to \(.65\) grid at increments of \(.025\), with the exact \(8/17\) objective anchor inserted, for 16 fitted points. The \(0\) and \(.80\) values are endpoint anchors outside the fit.

For each learner, the prespecified response is one when deterministic `argmax` classifies the single good-context authentic arm as the final-window modal arm, with modal ties permitted, and zero otherwise. The cell response is the across-learner fraction. This is an eight-action modal-classification fraction in the flat architecture, not a two-action softmax probability.

Within each architecture and master, the same policy/environment seed is reinitialized at every \(bf\) value, creating a common-random-number response curve. The flat and hierarchical architectures have separate seed families. The five master curves are the top-level replication units.

The learned crossing is defined operationally as the \(bf\) at which the fitted response crosses 0.5. First, the five cell responses at each \(bf\) are averaged with equal weight. A nonincreasing pool-adjacent-violators algorithm is then applied with equal point weights. If the fitted curve contains a plateau exactly at 0.5, the midpoint of the plateau's \(bf\) range is used. Otherwise, the crossing is linearly interpolated between adjacent fitted points that straddle 0.5. A crossing is estimable only if it lies strictly inside \((.30,.65)\). A response already below 0.5 throughout the tested range is classified `no_crossing_below`, interpreted as left-censored, and is not extrapolated.

The monotonic fit is an estimator-level constraint, not a claim that every finite-sample master curve is monotone or that the underlying learning process has been proven monotone in \(bf\). Equal point weights give each tested cost coordinate the same influence after master averaging. The inserted analytic value \(8/17\) is a tested anchor, not a target supplied to the fitting algorithm, and the response level 0.5 is a fixed reporting convention rather than a value derived from the objective crossover. This separation prevents the analytic benchmark from mechanically determining the learned crossing.

Uncertainty is calculated by 10,000 top-level bootstrap resamples. Each resample draws five complete master curves with replacement, averages them, refits the nonincreasing curve, and inverts it using the same rule. The 2.5th and 97.5th linear percentiles of valid crossings form the interval, provided at least 95% of bootstrap resamples yield valid in-range crossings. Otherwise the crossing is not treated as estimable. The hierarchical analysis follows the same rule; a below-range response remains left-censored with no extrapolated threshold.

The 0.5 learned crossing is a conventional operational summary of the simulated population response. It is neither the analytic objective crossover nor a psychological threshold. No binary-softmax identity governs it, and the design does not isolate why it may differ from \(8/17\). Temperature, multi-action normalization, endogenous sampling, constant-step learning, context discovery, and representation remain possible contributors. A planned \(T=5000\) extension was not executed.

## Reproducibility and artifact provenance

Every standard configuration records the payoff and learner settings, \(n\), \(T\), final window, master seeds, and design cells. The experiment packages write child-level or cell-level machine-readable data, summaries, reports, figures, and manifests containing the configuration, software environment, Git state, seed hierarchy, output paths, and content hashes. Deterministic publication tables and figures are generated from the preserved summaries rather than by rerunning the experiments.

Artifact identity and model-semantic identity are intact, but historical generator-source identity is not uniform. Fingerprint-exact source bundles were recovered for E04 and E05. For E01–E03, the preserved outputs are content-addressed and verified against their manifests, and the maintained model code matches the documented semantics, but the exact dirty-worktree generator bytes used for the historical runs cannot be reconstructed from repository history. Byte-exact regeneration of those three historical runs is therefore not currently possible. This is a bounded reproducibility limitation; it does not alter the preserved artifacts and is not treated as a reason to generate new evidence in this study.

## Scope of the modelling framework

The framework isolates selected learning and social structures rather than reproducing a complete human social environment. The reward labelled “objective” is objective only within a specified model and is not synonymous with welfare. The full-information comparators know the model and are intentionally asymmetric with the finite learners. Exact numerical parameters are normalized reference settings, not fitted estimates of gifted-child psychology, and tested grids do not define probability distributions over people or environments.

Relevance to gifted masking lies at the level of controlled abstraction. The actions represent degrees of visibility, the acceptance event represents one stylized form of social response, and the context models represent variation in receptivity and comparison burden. None of those correspondences identifies a clinical construct, developmental mechanism, diagnosis, or recommended intervention. The formal contribution is instead to show how those ingredients can be separated into inspectable computational questions with distinct comparators. Any transfer from the model to educational or psychological interpretation must preserve that distance.

Several structural choices require particular conditionality. For this first abstraction, M1 assumes an exactly linear disclosure-acceptance relation and independent tabular disclosure values. The reference M2 assumes deterministic entry to Stigmatized following a rejected Neutral authentic action, redemption probability exactly \(p/2\), and full-payoff restoration to Accepted after redemption. The hierarchical M3 learner credits both its context and behaviour values with the same realized reward. These choices define the models analysed here; they are not asserted to be uniquely justified or empirically established. Results obtained by varying \((p,\eta)\), \(K\), or \(bf\) establish behaviour only over those tested axes while all unswept quantities remain reference coordinates.

The methods therefore support bounded existence, structural, and tested-region comparisons. They do not provide empirical calibration, human prevalence, a natural measure over parameter space, a complete formal characterization of learner-objective disagreement, an information-matched optimum, or evidence of irreversible concealment. These limits define the intended scientific object rather than weakening it: simplified models make the distinction between finite learned behaviour and a precisely defined full-information objective benchmark inspectable.
