## Figure 1. Model progression and analytic structure.

The four nested models of the Article-V1 programme and the exact result that fixes
each one's objective benchmark. **M0** compares binary authenticity against masking
under static expected reward, whose gap Delta_0(p) = a + c − (1 − p)(b + r) vanishes
at the static M0 objective boundary p* = 43/60. Adding a graded action space gives
**M1**, whose expected reward E[R(d)] = b − c + d[a + c − (1 − p)(b + r)] is affine in
the disclosure level d, so every strict static optimum lies at an endpoint. Adding
persistent social state gives **M2**, in which the Neutral-state gap decomposes as
D_N(V) = Delta_0(p) + gamma[p V_A + (1 − p) V_S − V_N] and the comparator becomes the
exact full-information dynamic optimum. Adding heterogeneous receptive contexts gives
**M3**, where the full-information optimum is invariant in the number of contexts K
under the frozen one-good-context design, and the objective comparison-cost crossover
is exactly bf* = 8/17. The figure is analytic; it contains no simulation data. The
static M0 boundary p* = 43/60 is not the M2 dynamic threshold.

(95% CI [-0.035753, -0.015922]). The second row is
the *same* estimand expressed in normalized objective-loss units, linked by the exact

confirmation. The final-window mask-rate contrast is
-0.000343 with interval
[-0.011605, 0.010918], which spans zero.
That is a failure to detect persistent masking, not an established equivalence: E01 does

**(b) E02, M0/M1 binary versus graded.** Architecture contrasts for the approved

post-rejection nonzero disclosure by 0.255040
([0.253004, 0.257077]) while
lowering full-authentic final-window selection by
0.328692, lowering the probability that the

## Figure 3. Persistent-state learner/objective contrast map.

**E03, M2 regime replication**, frozen 21 × 17 baseline exact grid over acceptance
probability p and Stigmatized-to-Neutral recovery eta; 357 tested parameter-grid cells,
n = 160 learners per cell, T = 1000, five master seeds. **(a)** Each tested cell is
classified by comparing aggregate final-window Neutral-state action counts against the
exact full-information dynamic optimum in the same cell:
243 of 357 tested cells show learned-suboptimal
masking, 107 of 357 show the learner matching the
objective policy, and 7 of 357 are inconclusive because
Neutral visits failed the prespecified visit-adequacy rule. Inconclusive cells are shown,
not dropped. The black contour marks where the full-information optimum switches to
authenticity. Because the learner masked in the Neutral state in every cell whose visits
were adequate, the contrast region coincides with the region where the exact optimum is
authenticity. **(b)** The exact full-information Neutral Q gap Q(N, A_u) − Q(N, M) over the
same grid, with its zero contour, showing how strongly the objective comparator favours
authenticity across the contrast region. The gap is strongly asymmetric, spanning about
−6.65 to +0.85, so the diverging colour scale is anchored at zero and scaled independently
on each side; the colour bar carries the true values. These counts are **tested-grid coverage** under a
frozen learner: 243/357 means
243 of the 357 tested parameter-grid cells. It is
not a prevalence, not a share of people or environments, not phase-space volume, and not a
probability that masking occurs. The comparator is full-information and is not matched to
the learner's information.

## Figure 4. Receptive-context discovery and comparison-cost displacement.

**(a) E04, M3 search architectures.** Mean fraction of learners whose final-window modal
action is authenticity in a receptive context (`mean_settled_any_good_auth_argmax`), against
the number of contexts K, with one fixed receptive context. Lines are the mean over five
master seeds; shaded bands show the observed range across those seeds and are a descriptive
spread, not confidence intervals. The inset carries the primary frozen comparison, K = 16
minus K = 1, with Student-t intervals across five master seeds: flat 2K
-0.234000 ([-0.246417,
-0.221583]) and hierarchical -0.470400
([-0.511031, -0.429769]). Controls establish
that this is not a trivial K-only reward effect: the known-good control has no context
choice and is K-invariant by construction, the global-mask control moves by
0.008800, and holding receptive contexts at a fixed
proportion of K largely removes the decline. Under the frozen one-good-context design the
full-information optimum is itself K-invariant, because K changes multiplicity rather than
the distinct reward values available to a fully informed agent. As receptive contexts become
scarcer and must be discovered under finite sampling, learned authenticity can decline
substantially even though the objective optimum does not change. More contexts do not
intrinsically cause masking.

**(b) E05, M3 comparison-cost crossover.** Across-child fraction whose final-window modal arm
is the single receptive-context authentic arm, in a flat eight-action (2K, K = 4) policy,
against comparison cost bf over the approved dense range. Points are the equal-n pooled mean
over five master seeds; bands show the range across those seeds. The learned response crosses
0.5 at bf = 0.312852 (master-seed bootstrap
[0.307836, 0.316918], 10,000 resamples), well before the exact objective
crossover bf* = 8/17 = 0.470588; the displacement is
0.157736, and at the learned
crossing receptive-context authenticity still retains about
0.134076 of objective advantage. The
hierarchical response was already below 0.5 at the lower dense boundary
(status `no_crossing_below`), so no in-range crossing is estimable; it is
left-censored below the observed range and no threshold is extrapolated for it. This result
is descriptive: the design does not isolate a mechanism, and fixed softmax temperature,
action-set normalization, and hot-stove sampling remain possible contributors to the
displacement.
