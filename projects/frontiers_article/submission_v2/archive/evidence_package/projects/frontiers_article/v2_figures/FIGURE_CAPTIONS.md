# Article V2 — Main figure captions

## Figure 1. Underidentification architecture and experimental mechanism map.

**(A)** Observed ability visibility or concealment is modelled as an outcome of learning history, current social conditions, action representation, persistent social consequences, and the availability and discoverability of receptive contexts. These inputs shape the learner's information and expectations; distinct upstream configurations can therefore converge on similar observed concealment, while similar current conditions can yield different behaviour when histories and expectations differ. **(B)** E01–E05 decompose five such routes: restricted sampling after early feedback (E01), action representation (E02), persistent social state and dynamic value (E03), contextual discoverability (E04), and learned versus environmental boundaries (E05). The diagram is conceptual and contains no numerical simulation result.

## Figure 2. Learning history and action representation alter sampled and discovered actions.

**(A)** E01 compares learners whose forced initial authentic action was rejected versus accepted. Rejection reduced subsequent authentic sampling by −0.025837 (95% CI [−0.035753, −0.015922]), whereas the final-window mask-rate contrast was −0.000343 (95% CI [−0.011605, 0.010918]); the latter interval spans zero. **(B)** Within the rejected stratum, the model's 5% forced-authentic-exploration and counterfactual-authentic-feedback manipulations reduced final-window masking by 0.046590 [0.033230, 0.059951] and 0.118135 [0.109683, 0.126587], respectively, relative to the rejection baseline. These are model information manipulations. **(C)** In E02's rejected stratum, five independently learned graded disclosure arms increased continued non-zero disclosure relative to binary resampling (+0.255040 [0.253004, 0.257077]) but reduced full-authentic final-window selection (−0.328692 [−0.342037, −0.315348]) and unique-modal full-authenticity (−0.221740 [−0.288755, −0.154724]). Intervals are Student-*t* intervals across five master seeds.

## Figure 3. Persistent social state and learned–objective disagreement.

**(A)** In E03, authentic action from Neutral can lead to Accepted or Stigmatized social states; turnover and recovery can return the environment to Neutral. The relevant full-information objective therefore includes immediate reward and the continuation value of subsequent social state. **(B)** The frozen $21\times17$ receptivity-by-recovery grid compares aggregate final-window Neutral-state action counts with the full-information dynamic oracle. Of 357 tested parameter cells, 243 are maintained learned–objective disagreement cells, 107 agree with the oracle, and seven are inconclusive: five had inadequate Neutral exposure in every master, while two had classifications from only one or two adequately visited masters and failed the four-of-five stability rule. Markers and gray levels distinguish the three categories; the contour marks the oracle's switch to authenticity. Thus, `243/357` denotes tested parameter cells—not simulated learners, human prevalence, or a population frequency.

## Figure 4. Context discoverability and learned–objective boundary displacement.

**(A)** E04 plots the primary one-good-context condition using each learner's deterministic final-window first-index `argmax` classification of a good-context authentic action. The $K=16$ minus $K=1$ effects are −0.234000 [−0.246417, −0.221583] for the flat $2K$ representation and −0.470400 [−0.511031, −0.429769] for the hierarchical representation; the fixed-known-good control is exactly zero and the global-mask control is +0.008800 [0.002090, 0.015510]. Shaded regions are descriptive ranges across five master seeds; the inset uses the corresponding Student-*t* intervals across those masters. **(B)** E05 plots the frozen flat eight-action ($K=4$) pooled response against comparison cost $b_f$, its non-increasing PAVA fit, and the response threshold of 0.5. The learned behavioural crossing is $b_f^L=0.312852$ (95% complete-master-curve bootstrap CI [0.307836, 0.316918]), below the exact environmental/objective crossover $b_f^*=8/17=0.470588$; their displacement is 0.157736. The hierarchical response is already below 0.5 at the lower tested boundary ($b_f=0.30$), so its crossing is left-censored below the tested range rather than absent. The horizontal axis is comparison cost, not elapsed time or attempts.
