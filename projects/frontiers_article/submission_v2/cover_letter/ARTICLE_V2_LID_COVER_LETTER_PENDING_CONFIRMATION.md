Dear Editors,

Please consider the full length article, “Ability Concealment Does Not
Uniquely Reveal the Environment: Learning and Social Opportunity in Gifted
Learners.”

The manuscript addresses an educational inference problem: similar observed
ability concealment can arise from differences in learning history, action
representation, persistent social consequences, contextual discoverability,
or current environmental conditions. The paper therefore separates observed
behaviour from the environmental and learned processes that may produce it.
Across five controlled reinforcement-learning experiments, it distinguishes
current reward, learned value, dynamic value, and the availability versus
discoverability of receptive opportunities.

The work fits *Learning and Individual Differences* because its central
question concerns how finite learning and environmental structure can generate
individual variation without requiring that observed behavioural differences
map directly to stable learner traits. The models are deliberately stylized
computational environments, not calibrated psychological replicas or clinical
claims. Their contribution is to make competing explanations explicit and to
identify the longitudinal and contextual measurements needed to distinguish
them empirically.

Source code is available at
https://github.com/mousomer/gifted-masking. A content-addressed archive of the
frozen E01-E05 publication evidence has been prepared; its verified persistent
identifier will be inserted after deposit and before submission.
