## 1. Stage verdict

Article V2 inherits sufficient frozen Article V1 E01–E05 evidence to support an
L&ID article organized around an inference problem: similar observed ability-
concealment behaviour can be produced by different learning histories and
decision structures, and differing learned behaviour can emerge under the same
specified environment. The data support this proposition **within the stylized
models**; they do not identify the mechanisms operating in any child, estimate
their prevalence, or establish psychological traits.

The reframe is feasible, but three gaps must remain visible during Stage 2:

1. The literature audit leaves formal-prior-art searches open for E03
   persistent-state structure, E04 scarce-receptive-context discovery, and the
   E02 graded-disclosure-specific contribution. These are novelty gaps, not
   missing-result gaps.
2. The terminology decision remains open: `masking` is not yet established as
   the universally preferred technical term in giftedness research. Use
   **ability concealment/visibility management** for the construct, and define
   model **masking** only as the low-visibility action.
3. Exact historical generator worktrees are unrecoverable for E01–E03. Their
   outputs are content-addressed and intact, but the original runs cannot be
   byte-exactly regenerated from Git alone. E04/E05 generator-source recovery
   is complete.

No evidence supports reintroducing E06/M4. Article V1 remains intact as the
source manuscript/evidence lineage; V2 Stage 1 does not create a new evidence
freeze, new experiment namespace, or new result hashes. No evidence supports
treating the current working manuscript or uncommitted presentation artifacts as
a higher authority than the frozen V1 matrix and artifacts.

## 3. Authoritative L&ID thesis and research question

### Research question

> Why can learners facing similar specified social opportunities develop
> different patterns of ability concealment, and why can similar observed
> concealment arise from different underlying learning processes?

### Thesis

> Within the E01–E05 learning environments, ability concealment is an
> underidentified behavioural phenotype: finite feedback history, independent
> action representations, persistent social consequences, contextual
> discoverability, and learned–objective boundary separation can yield different
> behaviour under the same specified environment, while similar low-visibility
> behaviour can arise through distinct model mechanisms.

The first sentence is supported directly by E01 (history), E01’s labelled
learner-side weighting sensitivity (conditional learner variation), E03
(persistent state), E04 (contextual discovery), and E05 (learned versus
objective boundary). The second sentence is a bounded cross-model synthesis,
not a claim that the mechanisms are empirically identified in gifted learners.

### Required narrowing

- Do not call `lambda` an established trait of sensitivity to negative outcomes.
  It is a learner-side loss-weighting parameter and a model source of
  identification ambiguity.
- Do not say that E04 establishes a general theorem that social contexts become
  harder to search as their number grows. It establishes a representation-
  dependent result under scarce receptive contexts in its tested design.
- Do not claim an E05 causal mechanism for the threshold gap.
- Do not use **multifinality** or **equifinality** as empirical developmental
  findings. They are useful model-level organizing terms only under the
  definitions in Section 9.

## 4. Conceptual hierarchy

| L&ID layer | Demonstrated in the corpus | Model assumption / boundary | Empirical analogue to measure, not infer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Learner and learning history** | E01: initial rejection reduced later authentic sampling by .025837 in the tested p=.75/.85 cells; E01 `lambda` sensitivity changes final masking at p=.65 with payoffs fixed | `lambda` is not calibrated or validated as a stable trait; the single-rejection effect does not establish persistent final masking | Experienced feedback sequence, perceived severity, subsequent sampling, and individual learning/exploration tendencies |
| **Behavioural/action-space structure** | E02: five independent graded actions raise nonzero disclosure but reduce full-endpoint use and modal endpoint discovery | Disclosure levels have independent Q values; there is no learned interpolation/generalization | Available disclosure forms, whether evidence transfers between them, and perceived exposure cost |
| **Persistent social consequences** | E03: exact dynamic objective boundary changes with escape; aggregate Neutral final-window behaviour can favour masking where the oracle favours authenticity | M2 transition probabilities and payoffs are stylized; comparator has full information | Persistence, reversibility, and observability of social standing; future consequences after visible expression |
| **Contextual search/discoverability** | E04: flat and hierarchical receptive-context authentic argmax use falls with K, while the one-good-context objective best value is K-invariant; controls limit interpretation | Context choice is an abstract repeated selection problem, not literal movement among peer groups | Number and proportion of receptive settings, their legibility, opportunity cost of testing, and information transfer across settings |
| **Learned versus objective behaviour** | E05 flat crossing .312852 precedes exact `bf*=8/17`; objective advantage remains positive at the learned crossing | Eight-action across-child modal classification is not a binary softmax probability; mechanism is unresolved | Perceived comparison cost, disclosure behaviour, feedback, and whether environment-level incentives match learned expectations |

### Multifinality and equifinality verdict

| Term | Verdict | Direct support | Strongest defensible wording |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Multifinality** | **RETAIN, MODEL-LEVEL ONLY** | E01 controlled feedback histories produce different subsequent sampling; E01 loss-weight sensitivity yields different masking with the same p=.65 environment; finite learners differ across random histories by construction | “The models demonstrate possible history- and learner-dependent divergence under the same specified payoff environment.” |
| **Equifinality** | **RETAIN, MODEL-LEVEL SYNTHESIS ONLY** | E01/E02/E03/E04/E05 each provide distinct routes to lower visibility or reduced authentic action; the models are not jointly fitted or causally decomposed | “Across distinct model structures, superficially similar reduced-authenticity behaviour can be compatible with different learning and decision-structure explanations.” |

Do not write that either pattern has been demonstrated longitudinally in gifted
children.
