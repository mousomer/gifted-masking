# Opening slide: phenomenon and mechanism

Checked 6 October 2026. The author requested an opening that distinguishes the documented existence of masking from an account of its mechanisms. The slide retains that purpose while narrowing two broad claims.

## Displayed wording

**How does masking develop?**

- Decades of research document ability concealment among gifted learners.
- Age-related patterns vary across studies and social settings.
- Which learning processes make masking emerge, persist, or recede?

The last line is the study's question, not a claim that previous researchers never proposed mechanisms. The simulations are not calibrated to chronological age.

## Source decisions

| Source | Supports | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Swiatek (1995), An Empirical Investigation of the Social Coping Strategies Used by Gifted Adolescents](https://doi.org/10.1177/001698629503900305) | Established empirical work on social coping among gifted adolescents | Does not establish a universal developmental decline |
| [Chan (2005), The Structure of Social Coping Among Chinese Gifted Children and Youths in Hong Kong](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ746277.pdf) | Age-group differences in the organization and interpretation of coping strategies; prior social-stigma accounts | Cross-sectional age comparisons do not identify within-person developmental causality |
| [Foust, Rudasill, and Callahan (2006), An Investigation Into the Gender and Age Differences in the Social Coping of Academically Advanced Students](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ753971.pdf) | Concealment and age differences in an academically advanced summer-program sample | Older adolescents reported greater hiding and denial in this sample, contrary to a blanket claim that masking wanes with age |

The age evidence does not justify the unrestricted wording “masking wanes with age.” Decline may be a hypothesis or a finding for a specific sample, period, or coping measure, but would require that source and qualification. Concealment, denial of giftedness, and conformity are related but distinct measures.

The repository's [literature audit](../ARTICLE_V1_LITERATURE_AUDIT.md) identifies hot-stove learning, endogenous sampling, and richer feedback as established mechanisms. It also describes giftedness-specific stigma and coping theories. Consequently, the slide asks which explicit learning processes can reproduce emergence, persistence, or decline; it does not claim absence of all prior mechanism discussion or introduce a new novelty claim.

## Spoken opening: 35 seconds

Decades of research document gifted learners concealing ability and managing its visibility. Studies also examine age and context, although the direction of age differences is mixed. Existing theories discuss social coping. Our question is more specific: which learning processes can make masking emerge, persist, or recede? We use reinforcement-learning simulations to separate feedback, persistent social consequences, and opportunity structure. The aim is an explicit computational explanation of how behaviour can change over experience.
